# Generative Modeling

Artificial Intelligence, Generative Modeling. Models that learn a data distribution and draw new samples from it, one note per family of models.

- [[Diffusion Models]]: noising data in one jump to any level, composing two models by multiplying them and dividing out their shared background, and why adding two models' scores misses the noised product under the reverse process at any number of levels while annealed MCMC reaches it, and a model that outputs one energy per input, whose height lets a sampler accept or reject each move, holding the right width at any step size where unadjusted Langevin runs away past 2 / P, and lets two models be mixed; and the three ways to combine models, product (AND), mixture (OR) and negation (NOT), of which a score-only model can do the first and last but not the mixture; the kinds of composition (co-occurrence, intersection, interaction) and which variables each expert scores; and what an expert learns from labelled data, its training set's density, with labels mattering only where a conditioning rule or a classifier reads them; and why a product reaches a combination neither training set holds only where each expert has density there, with a four-quadrant answer key giving the exact share each way round it reaches (fine-tuned experts, one conditional model, experts that model only their own part); and the three numbers this literature calls annealed, the temperature that raises heights to a power, the noise level that moves samples, and the guidance scale that moves mass toward a condition; and why a tempered density's score is $\beta$ times the old score with no normaliser, and why $\beta = 0$ has no density, so a schedule starts above 0 or bends from a base; and the log of a power and of a quotient, which turn a tempered density's log into a scaled log minus one constant.
