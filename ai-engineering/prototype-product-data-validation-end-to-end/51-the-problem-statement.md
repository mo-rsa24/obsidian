# 51: The problem statement

## The statement

**The harm.** Customers choose online products from the page's name, images and description, without holding the pack. On the Dis-Chem storefront on 2026-09-30, a snack's title named one flavour while its pack image showed another, and its brand line named a different company from the title and pack. A protein powder's title shortened both its brand and its flavour, and a serum's title misspelt its brand and its product word. In each case the page makes it harder to find or identify the product. Whether anyone bought the wrong item is not established.

**The decision and who makes it.** The prototype supports a reviewer deciding what needs correcting before a product is published, and whether to accept a suggested fix. The proposed primary reviewer is the content team, with regulated content (warnings, ingredients, dosage) routed to a pharmacy or compliance owner. Both owners are proposals; Dis-Chem must confirm them and their authority. Every flag shows the BRS requirement it tests and the evidence behind it, so a reviewer can act without re-deriving it (§11).

**What success means.** I propose catching identity conflicts first: an image showing another product or variant, and a title that misnames the brand or product. These reach the customer and §11 lists an image that does not match the product as a hard block. Catching more of them will also flag more correct pages, and rules that fire on a whole category (medicine placeholders, Title Case names) could flood reviewers, so success counts false alarms as well as escapes. The acceptable escape rate and review load are Dis-Chem's decision; a labelled evaluation set, labelled without the model's suggestions, will provide the evidence.

**Scope.** The prototype works with three extracts together: Amplience (images), Silo (online content) and SAP BW (the ERP record). It tests the BRS checks these sources can support and reports every requirement they cannot support in a coverage and gap report. The BRS also assumes Sales Layer PIM, Commerce Cloud and Magento; these extracts do not demonstrate those integrations. The drug schedule and NAPPI code the storefront shows come from SAP's `SCHEDULE_ZA` and `NAPPI_CODE_ZA` (they matched the site for one traced product); 9,879 products carry a schedule from 1 to 6 and 23,026 a NAPPI code. Publishing to production and writing corrections back to source systems are outside the proposed scope.

**What it checks.** Categories and brands, including a new BMC or brand with no category (§3.1). Variants: attributes populated and consistent, each variant mapped to its base, and a discontinued base with an active variant (§3.2). Images: dimensions, file type, background, file size, aspect ratio, whether the image matches the product, and whether the filename's barcode matches the record (§§3.3, 3.4, 4.1); images needing resizing or compression are reported, and the change waits for approval. Names and descriptions against §5.1's rules, with suggested names and descriptions drafted from checked facts and sent for approval (§§3.5, 8). Pack text read by OCR, with warnings, ingredients and usage checked where they apply and routed to the compliance owner (§§8, 11). A scorecard of completeness, accuracy, consistency and media richness by product, category and brand (§§3.6, 9).

**What the design keeps.** Configurable rules by field, category, product type, image type and stage (§7); exceptions grouped by severity and required action (§§7 and 8); human approval of every AI-generated description and recommended attribute (§8); an audit log of each run's data version, rule version, time, errors, actions and outcome, and re-runs compared with the previous run (§7). It produces §9's five outputs: the validation result file, the exception report, the coverage and gap report, the product data scorecard and the audit log. A missing input stays a visible gap.

**Principles borrowed, to be tested here.** Settle the category first, then check that category's attributes (Shopify). Send the answers the model doubts to a person (Instacart PARSE). Draft descriptions only from facts already extracted and checked (attributes-first generation). Choose rules or a model per check (the rules-against-LLMs comparison). Python supplies the deterministic checks, ADK orders the workflow, Databricks Model Serving supplies model calls through LiteLLM, Delta and Unity Catalog hold the governed data, and MLflow records evaluation. Local evidence must justify each technique, wrong-image detection above all.
