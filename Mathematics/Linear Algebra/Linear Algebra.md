# Linear Algebra

Up: [[Mathematics]]

Mathematics, Linear Algebra.

**Linear algebra is the mathematics of vectors and the linear maps between them. A vector is a list of numbers read as an arrow, a matrix is a grid of numbers that moves vectors, and nearly every model in machine learning is built from these two and the dot product.**

- [[Vector]]: A vector is an ordered list of numbers, read as an arrow from the origin, that can be added to another vector and scaled by a number.
- [[Dot Product]]: The dot product of two vectors multiplies them entry by entry and adds the results, which equals their two lengths times the cosine of the angle between them.
- [[Norm]]: A norm measures a vector's size; the Euclidean norm, its length, is the square root of the sum of its squared entries.
- [[Orthogonality]]: Two vectors are orthogonal when their dot product is zero, meaning they are perpendicular; orthonormal vectors are orthogonal and also have length 1.
- [[Projection]]: Projecting a vector onto a direction splits it into a shadow along that direction and a remainder perpendicular to it.
- [[Cosine Similarity]]: Cosine similarity is the cosine of the angle between two vectors, their dot product divided by both lengths, so it compares direction and ignores length.
- [[Matrix]]: A matrix is a rectangular grid of numbers, and multiplying a vector by it applies a linear map: each output entry is the dot product of one row with the vector.
- [[Eigenvalue]]: An eigenvector of a square matrix is a direction the matrix only stretches, Av = λv, and the eigenvalue λ is the stretch factor.
- [[Singular Value Decomposition]]: The singular value decomposition writes any matrix as A = UΣVᵀ: a rotation, a stretch along perpendicular axes by the singular values, and another rotation.
