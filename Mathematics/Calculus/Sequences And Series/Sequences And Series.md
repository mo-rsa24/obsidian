# Sequences And Series

Mathematics, Calculus, Sequences And Series. **A sequence is a list of numbers that goes on forever, and a series is what you get by adding one up term by term.** The questions here are what a list closes in on as it goes, and how a function can be written as an endless sum whose first few terms are enough to compute with. Read the two notes in order: the second uses the first's series for $\log(1 + y)$.

- [[Maclaurin Series]]: writing a function as a polynomial that never ends; the series for $e^x$ worked at $x = 0.5$ to 1.648698, the series for $\cosh x$ from the odd terms cancelling, the series for $\log(1 + y)$, and $\log\cosh x = \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^4}{12}$ composed from them, each with the slip that breaks it.
- [[The Limit That Defines E]]: why $(1 + \frac{a}{n})^n$ settles on $e^a$ (2.716924 at $a = 1$, $n = 1000$) through $n\log(1 + \frac{a}{n}) = a - \frac{a^2}{2n} + \cdots$, why an extra term that shrinks faster than $\frac1n$ changes nothing, and why "the base goes to 1" is wrong.
