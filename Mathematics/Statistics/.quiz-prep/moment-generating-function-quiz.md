## Words this uses

Navigation: ⬅️ [Position](#position) | 📋 [Table of contents](#table-of-contents) | [What the grades rest on](#what-the-grades-rest-on) ➡️

**Covered** (node verdict): at least one kind holds a passage graded full. No plan task that teaches these nodes is ticked, so whether the learner already holds any of them is the learner's to say, and this file does not claim it.

**Gap**: no passage in any kind is graded full. A question whose tree holds a gap is blocked.

**Covered (source read)**: a textbook question's own node (N32 to N37). It is covered when its problem was read at the cited page and the question has a solution, the book's or one the quiz writes. The preparation for it is the concept nodes beneath it, graded like any other.

**Tier**: the learner has set no phase goal, so every node is marked `needed now`. That is a default, set once here for all 31 questions. With a goal, some nodes would move to `when it bites` or `link only` and the short path would be shorter than the full path.

**Minutes** (the `Min` column): estimates. For a node graded full they are the words of the passage that teaches it, at 120 words a minute, plus 5 minutes for the worked example by hand, rounded. For a gap they are the length the missing passage is expected to have. A question's total is the sum of its rows, so a node shared by several questions is counted in each question's total.

**Full**: all five parts of the format's standard are present: what and why in plain words, a worked example with every intermediate value, the method as steps, one failure with its number, and a check. Steps are accepted as an ordered chain of lines each with its value, or as a script a reader can rerun on new numbers. **Partial** names the missing part. **Mention** means named or used without being taught. **None** means no passage.

**The note's own numbers**: the quiz's stems use made-up numbers, as the note does. A preparation passage teaches the method on other numbers, so it never prints a question's answer.
